# FilmBuddy Vision

## Why FilmBuddy Exists

Film photography has a beautiful problem: the gap between shooting and seeing your results. Days or weeks pass between pressing the shutter and holding the developed negatives. By then, you've forgotten the details that would help you learn and improve.

- Which camera has the Portra, and which has the HP5?
- Was frame 12 the one where I bracketed a stop over, or was that frame 13?
- Which lens did I have on for those street shots?
- Where exactly was that photo taken?

Existing solutions either demand too much (log every single shot with full metadata) or offer too little (generic notes apps with no structure). Film photographers need something in between: a field companion that captures just enough, just fast enough, to bridge that gap between shooting and learning.

## Who Is FilmBuddy For?

**Experienced film photography enthusiasts** who:

- Shoot regularly and own multiple cameras
- Care about improving their craft through reflection
- Want to remember intentions and experiments, not just results
- Find existing apps either too tedious or too limited
- Value their shooting flow and won't tolerate fiddly interfaces

These are photographers who already have habits and opinions. They know their gear. They don't need hand-holding—they need a sharp tool that respects their time.

## What FilmBuddy Does

### Core Workflow: Roll-Centric Management

Everything revolves around **active rolls**. You're not logging shots—you're managing film that's currently loaded in your cameras and occasionally noting things you'll want to remember.

**Loading a roll:**

- Select a camera (or add a new one)
- Select a film stock (or add a new one)
- Set the shooting ISO (for push/pull processing)
- The roll appears in your active list

**While shooting:**

- Slide to the current frame number when you want to log something
- Choose what to note: lens change, exposure settings, GPS location, meter reading
- Only log what matters—most frames need no notes at all

**Finishing a roll:**

- Mark it as finished
- It moves to your historical archive, fully searchable

### Smart Light Meter

A light meter that actually works the way photographers think:

- Set any two values (e.g., aperture and shutter speed), get the third (ISO is already known from the roll)
- Lens-aware: knows your 35mm Nokton goes from f/1.5 to f/16, not the standard f/1.4
- Camera-aware: only suggests shutter speeds your camera actually has
- Can also run standalone for quick light checks without attaching to a roll

### Your Gear Library

FilmBuddy remembers your equipment:

- **Cameras:** Name, film format (35mm, 120), available shutter speeds
- **Lenses:** Name, specific aperture values, compatible camera mounts
- **Film stocks:** Name, native ISO, format

Once added, your gear is always a tap away. The app knows that your Leica M6 takes 35mm and can use your Summicron or your Nokton.

### Historical Archive

Every finished roll is preserved. Browse by camera to answer questions like:

- What have I shot on my Hasselblad this year?
- When did I last use Tri-X?
- What were my notes from that trip to Portland?

## What FilmBuddy Doesn't Do (For Now)

- **Track post-shooting workflow:** No "awaiting development" or "scanned" status
- **Edit historical notes:** What you logged is what you get
- **Social features:** This is a personal tool, not a community
- **Film stock reviews or databases:** You know what you like
- **Scanning or image management:** Other apps do this well

## Design Principles

### Field Notebook Feel

Quick, utilitarian, gets out of your way. But a field notebook can still have personality—FilmBuddy should feel like a trusted companion, not sterile software.

### Selective by Design

The value is in what you _choose_ to log, not in completeness. Most frames need no notes. The app should make logging fast when you need it and invisible when you don't.

### Opinionated Over Flexible

We make decisions so you don't have to. One clear way to do things, optimized for the common case. Flexibility can come later if needed.

### Gear-Aware Intelligence

Your equipment has specific capabilities. The app should know that your lens stops at f/1.5, that your camera's fastest shutter is 1/1000, that 6x7 gives you 10 frames. This awareness makes every interaction smarter.

### Respect the Flow

Photography is about being present. Every second spent in the app is a second not spent seeing. Interactions should be measured in moments, not minutes.

## The FilmBuddy Personality

FilmBuddy has a character (see the icon). It's a companion, not just a tool. Friendly but not chatty. Helpful but not intrusive. The kind of presence you'd want on a long photo walk—there when you need it, quiet when you don't.
